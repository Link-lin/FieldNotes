# Trip page v1 — trip view, day map, costs and the actions each role has

**Status:** First-release design, as built. Subordinate to [`PRD.md`](../../PRD.md) (TRIP-1–10, MAP-1–9, BUDGET-4, BUDGET-6–7, ACCESS-8–11) and the [technical design](technical-design.md#trip-page-and-day-map). Last revised 2 Oct 2026.
**Reference:** the interactive "Field Notes Atlas" prototype (private Claude artifact, sample data) shows the look. This document and the PRD govern behavior and data; the prototype's floating-point money, in-memory data, stand-in sign-in and Google Fonts loading do not carry over.

## Visual direction

A warm paper-and-ink look taken from the two reference repositories. It is light only. The Figma screens still show an earlier navy and lake-blue treatment and need updating.

| Token | Value | Use | Contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | `#FAF5E8` | Page background, with a very faint paper grain | — |
| Card | `#FFFDF6` | Cards, tiles, dialogs | — |
| Line | `#E4D9BF` | Dividers and decorative borders only | decorative |
| Control border | `#8E8066` | Input, select and filter borders | 3.5:1 on paper |
| Ink | `#3A3026` | Text, primary buttons, timeline | 11.8:1 |
| Ink 2 | `#675C4C` | Secondary text | 6:1 |
| Ink 3 | `#766A55` | Small labels, help text, times | 4.9:1 on paper, 5.2:1 on card |
| Vermilion | `#DE4F2A` | Fills, markers, bars and large display text only (3.7:1) | not for small text |
| Vermilion ink | `#A63615` | Vermilion text, and the fill behind white text (numbers, danger buttons) | 6.1:1 on paper; white on it 6.6:1 |
| Vermilion soft | `#F8DACD` | Tag and highlight backgrounds | — |
| Forest | `#516B55` (soft `#DDE6D6`) | Booked state and under-budget bar only | — |
| Ticket blue | `#DCEBEE` / `#23414B` | Flight boarding-pass card | 8.9:1 |

Cost breakdown colors by type are neutral inks and blues; they never reuse vermilion or forest, which mean booking and budget state.

Type: Source Serif 4 for headings and figures, IBM Plex Mono for small uppercase labels, the system sans for body text. Fonts are self-hosted (no Google Fonts request). Pill buttons, 24 px card radius. Interactive targets are at least 24 × 24 px, and at least 44 px tall on touch screens for primary actions. **Decision to confirm:** [`atlas-v1.md`](atlas-v1.md) rejected a green theme. Forest is used only as a small semantic accent, never as a brand or surface color.

## Pages and states

**Sign-in.** A single card with **Continue with Google**, plus the ACCESS-9 states. Denied says "This Google account is not set up for Field Notes. Try another account, or ask the trip owner for an invitation." and offers **Switch Google account**. Wrong account says "This invitation is for a different Google account" and offers a switch-account action. Nothing behind the card renders until a session exists; a deep link is kept as the redirect target.

**Dashboard.** When trips exist, a compact **Your trips** heading sits above **New trip** and **Create from an AI plan**, followed by short "Travelling now" (day *N* of *M*) and "Next departure" (days to go) links. The filters follow directly; they carry the counts, while the list's accessible heading is visually hidden to avoid repeating the page title. The empty dashboard keeps the larger introduction. The globe and synchronized list follow [Atlas v1](atlas-v1.md); on narrow screens, an existing trip list comes before the globe. Trip cards offer **Open trip**, a globe action, and a relative-time label ("in 50 days", "day 5 of 8"). An owned card with pending bookings has one compact link showing the total and overdue count; it opens that trip's **Bookings** view. The dashboard does not repeat the task rows. Events are added, edited and deleted only on the trip page.

**Trip page** (`/trips/[tripId]`). Changing tabs replaces the address rather than adding history. Escape returns to the dashboard only when no menu, dialog or text field has focus. Top to bottom:

The page uses the available viewport width rather than a fixed content column, inside side margins that grow with the window from 16 px on phones to 72 px on wide screens (the app header uses the same margins). The header spans the width, with the stamp at its right edge. From 1,061 px the itinerary and map sit side by side: the map column takes 30% of the window, between 340 and 560 px, and the timeline gets the rest. At 1,060 px and narrower, the map moves above the timeline and everything becomes one column. Text-heavy rows keep a readable measure inside those regions rather than stretching individual lines across the screen. Timeline grids shrink to the available width; long event titles, time zones and map-link labels wrap, and phone flight cards use smaller route codes. Map-edge pointers appear once the map width is measured, preventing a wider initial layout on phones.

1. Back to all trips, status and role tags, title, destination, a postmark stamp (city, start month and year, length), and the actions the viewer's role allows: **Add to itinerary** (owners and editors); **Edit trip** (which also holds **Delete trip**) and **Share** (owners only); and **Show on globe** (when the trip has a point; everyone). Viewers see only **Show on globe**. The role tag reads Owner, Editor or Viewer, followed by "shared by [creator]" for anyone who did not create the trip. **Import AI plan** would join the owner actions if TRIP-7 is approved. At 1,280 px and wider **Up next** sits on the header's right as a few lines of text (no box), with the stamp beside it at the page edge; both are centred on the title block so they line up: before the trip, its first dated event; during it, the next event today (skipping ones that have started) or else tomorrow's first; after it, nothing. Its small top line gives the day and start time (with the city when the event's zone is not the trip's), then the title on its own line, then place and booking state; it opens the event like a timeline row, and ends with the dated flights as one route line ("SFO → HNL → OGG → KOA → SFO"). Narrower layouts leave it out, since the timeline shows the same events. The header becomes compact and drops the decorative stamp and Up next in Bookings so the tasks appear sooner.
2. A persistent **Itinerary / Bookings** switch immediately below the header. Bookings shows the pending count and, when applicable, the overdue count. The default is Itinerary. The booking view has a stable `?view=bookings` address; switching views replaces the current history entry.
3. In Itinerary, two compact highlights (TRIP-4) show time to departure, day *N* of *M* or time since the trip ended, and planned cost when a budget or prices exist. They sit beside the Itinerary/Bookings switch and wrap below it when needed. Bookings does not show them. Event and pin counts remain in the day tabs; distances remain in the map stop list. On phones the highlights wrap normally instead of adding another horizontal strip.
4. Itinerary day navigation: at 600 px and narrower, a labelled native **Day view** picker and 44 px Previous/Next buttons replace the scrolling tab strip. The picker includes **Whole trip**, every date and outside-trip dates, with the selected day number, Today marker and event/pin counts or "Nothing planned" below it. Buttons stop at either end; Previous from the first date returns to Whole trip. Wider screens use a thin, rounded scrollbar in the control-border colour on a transparent track, with ARIA tabs and arrow keys: **Whole trip** (default), then one per date in the trip range, plus **Outside trip dates** tabs when needed. Each shows the trip-day number, event and pin counts, and a **Today** marker. Empty days say "Nothing planned". Switching tabs eases the tab colours (0.28 s) and glides the strip to keep the chosen tab in view; the new timeline fades up (0.32 s), with a 28 px sideways slide only above 600 px, its first eight events settle in 35 ms apart, and the map card's contents settle as it changes. Reduced motion switches instantly.
5. In Itinerary, two columns: day sections with the timeline, then **Undated flights** and **Undated** in Whole trip; and the day map and stop list. The map column is sticky on wide screens, in Whole trip too, and never taller than the window (a long stop list scrolls inside it). **Planned costs** (budget currency first) and **Globe location** follow the timeline in the left column, so a short day does not leave an empty region under its events while waiting for the map to end. At the one-column breakpoint the map comes first. Events deleted during this visit appear under **Recently deleted** after the last day, each with **Restore**, for 10 minutes.
6. In Bookings, a focused checklist groups tasks as **Due now** (overdue or due today), **Coming up** and **No book-by date**. Each group is visible only when it has tasks. On wide screens, the explanation stays in a left rail and task rows use two columns inside each group. Empty trips and trips without pending tasks have distinct empty messages; viewers see the list read-only.

**Timeline rows.** A numbered marker matches the pin number, then time, title, a one-line place with one **Open in [provider]** pill (Google Maps when no link is saved), and tags for price, booking state and unverified AI drafts. Long place names shorten visually; opening the row shows the full place, type, notes and additional links in the event view (TRIP-10). Flights are a boarding-pass card with route codes and price; a flight with no airport times yet is a slim one-line card with only what is known ("OGG → KOA", or "Airports not set"), and a title that just restates the route is not repeated on the card. Rows without a time show no time line, since they sit under **Unscheduled**, **Undated** or **Undated flights**. An item without a pin says **Not on the map yet**. Owners and editors see a three-dot button at the top right of every event row, always visible, with room reserved so titles never run under it. It opens a menu with **Edit event** and **Delete event**: click, Enter or Space opens it, arrow keys move, and Escape, Tab or an outside click closes it and returns focus to the button. Delete takes effect at once and shows a toast with **Undo** that takes focus, stays until dismissed or at least 10 seconds, and pauses while hovered or focused; focus then moves to the next event's menu button, or to the day's add row. Each day ends with a dashed **Add an event to this day** row. A floating **Add to itinerary** button stays on screen for owners; toasts sit above it and never overlap it.

**Day map** (MAP-3 to MAP-9). A subtle grid overlays a bundled outline map with Natural Earth 1:10m coastlines and country borders, plus city labels when room permits. It is labeled "Outline map · no streets". Numbered pins, dashed same-day route lines (no flight legs), scale bar, north arrow, **+**/**−** buttons, wheel zoom only while the pointer is over the map (handing scrolling back to the page at its limits), pinch zoom, drag pan, a one-finger swipe on touch screens that scrolls the page, **Reset view** when zoomed, including zooming out to twice the initial view width; **+** zooms in to a view about 1 km across. Pins and outlines share one projection. **Start view** (MAP-3): it fits the stops on the ground and the flight arrivals within 300 km of them, less any stop more than 2,500 km from the middle of the rest and six times as far as most of them; at least 8 km across; widened, up to 40 km, to bring the nearest town label into view, or for a single place with no town that close to the full 40 km, so a summit or a beach shows the coast or island around it; and never widened so far that two separate places come within about 24 px. Town labels show by scale: every rank up to 80 km across, then fewer, down to the largest cities on a continent-wide view. A stop beyond the reachable map (half a map past the start view) appears as a small pill on the map's edge, on the line from the middle toward it: an arrow, its number and short name ("16 · SFO") and the straight-line distance from the nearest stop on the map ("3,753 km"); pills that would touch merge ("9·15 · 2 stops"). It sits under the zoom controls, labels give way to it, it lights with its row, and clicking it goes to that event like a pin; its accessible name adds the direction ("to the north-east, beyond this map"). Pins that nearly coincide, such as return visits to one place, merge into one marker listing their numbers ("2·9", or "2+3" for stop 2 and three more when they don't fit). Clicking it, or Enter or Space, flies in until its stops part when they are more than 150 m apart; for one venue it opens a small list beside it ("Same place · 4 stops") with each stop's number, name and day; choosing one goes to that event, and Escape, an outside click, panning or zooming closes it. Labels hide on collision. Below it, a stop list with straight-line distances ("18 km from 1"). The map card's first row holds the day and map kind on the left and, on a single day, the **Outline / Road route** switch and a small **Google Maps ↗** link (named "Open this day in Google Maps" for assistive technology) on the right; a note under the map says it opens this day's pinned locations. Hovering or focusing a timeline row, a stop-list row or a pin highlights all three (a marker for several stops lights for each of them); Enter or Space on a pin scrolls to and highlights its event. **Highlight zoom.** Resting on a timeline row or stop-list row for 0.2 s (or focusing one) flies the map to that stop: centred on it, at most 20 km across, tighter where a neighbouring place would otherwise share its marker (never narrower than 6 km, where the outline still shows land), so the marker that holds it lights. A stop beyond the map doesn't move it; its edge pill lights instead. Resting on a day heading, a day label in the stop list or a day tab flies to a view that holds that day's stops, at least 20 km across. A flight takes 0.45 to 1 s, longer for longer distances, eases in and out, and over a long way zooms out only a little (to about 40% of the distance, so a flight to an airport across an ocean doesn't take the map out to the whole trip) so the eye keeps its bearings. Moving to the next row flies on from where the camera is after 0.12 s, with no step back in between, and a stop already well placed in the view doesn't move it. Leaving everything for 0.3 s eases the map out to 2.5 times as wide, around the same place and never wider than the start view, and leaves it there: the map doesn't go back to the whole trip on its own. **Reset view** (shown whenever the view isn't the start view) flies there, smoothly. The pointer going onto the map holds what it shows for as long as it stays there, so the stop can be looked at, and the easing out happens once it leaves; zooming, panning, grabbing the map or **+**/**−** takes over for good, and a highlight then flies on from that view. Moving the pointer onto anything that points at nothing counts as leaving, so a row removed from under the pointer can't leave the map zoomed. Pins, touch screens (a tap's mouseover), a map out of sight and focus that came from a click rather than the keyboard don't move the map, and reduced motion jumps instead of flying. At local zoom the outlines remain approximate. On a day with pinned stops and an Embed API key, **Road route** switches to a user-opened Google iframe with driving or walking routes, Google’s markers, and the same Field Notes stop list below. Flight breaks or more than 22 stops create selectable route segments. **Outline** restores the custom pins and unloads Google. Switching days also resets to Outline. The road view explains that opening it sends that segment’s coordinates to Google; the outline sends none.

**Event view and editor** (TRIP-10, MAP-8). The whole row is clickable (one stretched details button under the row, also opened with Enter or Space; its links and three-dot menu stay above it and keep working). At widths above 600 px, an 820 px panel slides in from the right edge (0.38 s, fast-out ease, transform only) while a paper-coloured wash with a slight blur fades the trip page (0.3 s); closing reverses both in about a quarter of a second. At phone widths, the event opens as a full page at its own URL, with a return to the selected day or Bookings view. The sticky bar holds **‹ Previous**, **Next ›**, **Edit event** for the owner, and a close or trip-return control. Content: the day, date and time line with the stop number, title, type and booking tags, Google map (240 px, rounded, a quiet placeholder until it loads; "Map by Google. Opening an event sends its place to Google."), flight card for flights, place with its map link, booking and price, links and notes. The owner edits notes in the view. They save after a short pause in typing, when the field loses focus and when leaving the event, with Saving…, Saved and error states and a counter near 5,000 characters; viewers read them. Leaving through the view controls waits for a pending save; on failure it stays open and asks **Stay** (focused) or **Leave without saving**. **Edit event** changes the same surface into a form: editable large title, saved-place map with an update-after-save note, then Time & type, Booking & planned price, Place & map, and Notes. The editor saves explicitly and asks before discarding unsaved edits through its controls. On desktop, focus goes to the close button on open and back to the row on close; Escape and the faded page also close it. Reduced motion shows and hides it without movement.

**Highlight.** A soft vermilion wash and left accent edge behind the entry, a glow ring on its number, the time in vermilion ink, and a glowing pin. The flight card is never covered by a solid fill.

**Automatic pins** (MAP-2). With place lookup set up, an event added or renamed with a place name and no map link gets its pin a few seconds later when the lookup finds one clear match; on an open page it arrives as a live update ("Trip updated: 1 event pinned on the map."). Its event view says under the place "Pinned automatically from the place name. If it's the wrong place, Edit event changes or removes the pin." and its editor's map-link hint says how to move or clear it. Without a match the row keeps **Not on the map yet**. Without a lookup key the editor hides **Find place on map** and the hint does not mention automatic pins.

**AI drafts** (IMPORT-7). Owners and editors see, above the day tabs in Itinerary, an information band while any event is an unverified AI draft: "**3 events are AI drafts** that nobody has checked yet. Open one to check it, or mark them all reviewed once the plan looks right. Prices stay estimates until you confirm them in each event." with **Mark all reviewed** (**Mark it reviewed** for one), which marks exactly the drafts on screen and shows "3 events marked as reviewed." with **Undo** in the toast, as Delete does. A draft's row menu adds **Mark as reviewed** (with a check icon) between Edit and Duplicate, with the same toast; focus then returns to its menu button. The event view puts **Mark as reviewed** under the tags with "When you have checked it: the AI draft tag goes, the price stays an estimate."; it answers in place with "Marked as reviewed." and **Undo**, which takes focus, so nothing leaves the panel. A reviewed AI event shows no draft tag anywhere, and its event view lists "Origin: AI suggestion, reviewed". If an event changed after the page showed it, nothing is marked and the toast says so. Viewers see the tags only.

**Live updates** (TRIP-11). While the trip page, a phone event page or the dashboard is on screen it checks for changes every 3 seconds, and at once when the window gets focus or the connection comes back; a hidden tab checks nothing. A change refreshes the page in place: the open panel, day tab, scroll position, menus and anything typed stay. Rows and stop-list lines that were added or changed elsewhere take the highlight above for 2.4 seconds (pins are left alone), and the status toast says what changed in one line, "Trip updated: 2 events added, 1 changed." (also "pinned on the map" and "removed"; "Trip updated." for the trip's own details; "You can now only view this trip." and similar when a role changes). That note takes no focus and is dropped rather than replacing a toast with **Undo**. A change that lands within 2.5 seconds of this tab saving something is its own and says nothing new. On the dashboard a trip created or shared elsewhere appears with "New trip: [title]." An open event deleted elsewhere keeps its last details, read-only, under "This event was deleted elsewhere: in another tab or window, by someone you share the trip with, or by a connected chat." Notes keep saving across changes to the event's other fields; notes changed elsewhere replace the text only when nothing unsaved is typed, and otherwise the save reports the clash and keeps the text. Saving the editor over a change made meanwhile says so and keeps the edits in the form.

**Bookings** (BOOK-3, BOOK-4). Tasks are sorted by book-by date inside the urgency groups. An overdue task is in vermilion ink with a warning icon. A task's title opens its event: the side panel at widths above 600 px, or the event page on phones. Closing the panel returns focus to the task; the phone event page returns to Bookings, and one more browser Back returns to the prior page. Below each task the owner sees **Mark booked** (for a flight without both airports and times, a note to add them instead) and **Set book-by date** or **Change date**, which turns the book-by line into a date field with **Save**, **Remove date** and **Cancel**; Escape cancels. **Mark booked** removes the task and shows a toast with **Undo** that takes focus, as Delete does; Undo brings the task back with its date, and otherwise focus moves to the next task or the Bookings heading. Viewers see the tasks and can open them, with no actions.

**Costs** (BUDGET-6–7). One card per currency: code, number of prices, total, a bar for amount left of the budget (vermilion and "Over budget by …" when exceeded) only in the budget currency, a stacked bar and legend by type in neutral colors, and an "unverified AI estimate" note while any AI price remains. When several currencies exist, a note says they are never added or converted and only the budget currency counts toward the budget. Currency codes must be real ISO 4217 codes. Currency conversion is deferred (PRD roadmap).

## Dialogs

One modal pattern: focus trap with the page behind made inert, Escape and backdrop close, focus on the first field (or first button), focus returned to the trigger or its re-rendered equivalent, and error text tied to its field that moves focus there.

- **Add to itinerary / Edit event** (the same TRIP-9 fields): Add uses a dialog; its date is the day in view, or empty from Whole trip. Edit is pre-filled in the event panel or page above. For an item with imported links, **Use as map link** adopts one.
- **Edit trip / New trip:** title, destination, start and end dates, time zone, optional budget and currency. The time-zone picker lists the destination's zones and this device's zone first, then a short list of about 60 zones ordered by offset, with every IANA zone one step away. Event and flight-airport zones use the same picker. Changing dates warns about events that fall outside the new range. Edit trip ends with a **Delete trip** danger zone that opens a typed-name confirmation.
- **Create from an AI plan:** copy a prompt (the optional brief suggests a title from destination and dates), paste JSON v1 (a Markdown code fence is accepted), preview, confirm. The preview shows each item as a card: its title and tags, then its time, place and price on one line (a flight shows its airports with their local times, and a long place name is shortened so the price stays in view). A card opens to edit the item; cards with issues start open and reopen when **Review creation** finds problems, and **Expand all** and **Collapse all** above the list open or close every card. Date, time and date-and-time fields (trip dates, event dates and times, a flight's planned date and airport times) use the browser's pickers while their value is empty or valid; a value the picker can't show, such as an AI's "2027-02-30", stays as text in a plain field so nothing is hidden, and the picker returns when the fixed field loses focus. Items arrive as unverified drafts, never Booked, and never pinned. On a trip, **Import AI plan** appends items (TRIP-7, proposed).
- **Event side panel:** the same focus rules through the shared `useDialog` hook, but it slides in from the side and fades the page rather than dimming it. The phone event page uses normal page navigation (see Event view and editor above).
- **Share** (owners): a notice of what everyone invited can see and what each role can do (viewer, editor, owner), a choice of **By email** or **By link** with an address field or a name-or-note field and a role picker (viewer by default; the role's description sits beneath it), the one-time link shown once with a message that says what the role allows, and the list of people. A line above the list says who created the trip and that they are always an owner, or that its creator has deleted their account. Each person has a role picker (choosing owner asks "Make … an owner?" first, since owners can share and delete the trip), **Revoke** and **Create new link**; a revoked entry reads "Was editor" and so on.
- **Account menu:** name, email, roles, **Sign out**, **Sign-in methods** (when WeChat is set up: connect Google or WeChat to this account), **Delete my account** (one choice per owned trip: another owner keeps it, a new owner is chosen with the next in line suggested, or it is deleted for everyone; type DELETE).

## Motion

Everything here is decorative and off under reduced motion, including pseudo-element animations: page fade and rise, staggered cards, count-up numbers, one globe intro rotation under two seconds, a marker pulse on upcoming trips only, a slowly moving dashed globe ring and selection ring, pins popping in, the marching route line, timeline drawing itself, scroll reveal, stamp-in. Looping motion stops within 10 seconds of the last interaction. Status is always carried by shape, text or number as well.

## Acceptance checks

- An owner or editor reaches **Add** without scrolling on the trip page, and the three-dot menu with Edit and Delete on every event row, at desktop and phone widths; the dashboard shows no add, edit or delete controls for events; an editor sees no Edit trip or Share, and a viewer sees none of these controls.
- After Delete, focus is on **Undo**; after Undo or closing any dialog, focus is on a sensible control, never lost to the page body.
- Whole trip and each day show the same events and numbering; the pin numbers equal the timeline numbers; day numbers count from the trip start date.
- A Google Maps, Apple Maps or OpenStreetMap link with coordinates pins the stop; a shortened, Amap, Baidu, look-alike-host or imported link does not; the page makes no request to a tile, geocoding, map or font service until an event's side panel or the optional Road route opens, and then only the Google Maps Embed API frame for that view.
- A trip with two currencies shows two cost cards, no combined total, and a budget comparison only in the budget currency.
- Text meets 4.5:1 and control borders 3:1 against their background.
- With reduced motion set, nothing animates and nothing is hidden waiting for an animation.
- Keyboard: pins, tabs, menus, dialogs, toasts and the floating button are reachable, with visible focus.
